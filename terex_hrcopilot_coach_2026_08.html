Coaching Analysis: HR Copilot Fundamentals

Terex · 12 completed activities · August 18–19, 2026 · Flint Learning Solutions
How the activity works. Every session opens with the coach asking the participant how they currently handle a long document when someone needs an answer fast. That answer, quoted below as "Started," is the participant's own description of their process before using Copilot at all: usually reading or skimming the whole thing by hand.

The coach then helps the participant write a Copilot prompt with one required piece: an instruction telling Copilot to say exactly where in the source document it found each part of its answer (for example, "and tell me which page or section each point came from"). This report calls that the source-citation instruction. Without it, there is no way to check Copilot's answer against the original document before sending it. Almost every participant left this out on their first attempt.

The session ends with the participant picking 2–3 specific claims from Copilot's answer and checking those against the original document by hand, which is what "Ended" describes below.

  • Coach sequence, all 12 sessions: ask current method, attach a real document, build a seven-part prompt (who Copilot should act as, what a good answer looks like, the actual request, the format, an example, the background, the tone), then check 2–3 load-bearing claims against the source.
  • 9 of 12 first prompt drafts left out the source-citation instruction. The coach added it each time. Two participants (SV Corporate, US-WY Home; AWP USA, US-WA Moses Lake) included it unprompted.
  • Routing: Word or Outlook sidebar when the source is a document already open there (6 of 12), separate Copilot chat window for PDFs, exported files, or pasted text (6 of 12).
  • One session (SV AEV, US-NC Jefferson V02): before any prompt, the coach flagged real employee names in investigation notes and told the participant to swap them for role labels.

What the Coach Is Teaching Us

  • No participant described an existing verification habit before this activity. All 12 started from manual reading or skimming.
  • The recurring gap is one specific piece: the source-citation instruction. Missing in 9 of 12 first drafts.
  • One participant (AWP USA, US-WA Bothell, mobility pay) first named a subjective point to check; the coach redirected to the factual claim.
  • Two sessions show a records gap: one survey response contradicts its transcript, one completed session has no survey response.
  • One participant pasted unredacted employee names from an investigation file. The coach addressed that before any prompt-engineering guidance.
