Three we’ve learned and responded to.

~15
reports — the single most common gap
Name the gear and the person — general reminders didn’t land.
The most common gap was recognition and reminders that stayed general — “wear your gear” — with no specific gear, task, or person named. Where supervisors named the exact gear and person, it landed.
→ The refresh builds the specifics into what counts as done.
~38
activities — the most repeated thing done right
The end-of-shift check is where worn gear gets caught.
Supervisors caught worn-through gloves and scratched face shields at floor and end-of-shift checks and swapped them on the spot. The same check surfaced worn gear as the top barrier.
→ Keep the end-of-shift condition check central.
2026
the gap persisted into the second run
PPE comes off at transitions.
Protection comes off when the task changes or a break starts, and goes on late when work resumes — safety glasses pushed up to see a mark, the weld hood left up for a quick tack, ear plugs left out after lunch. The 2026 conversations show the same pattern.
→ Target the transition moments: on before the hazard, back on after the break.

How we got these numbers

Two kinds of figure appear in this report and they are never added together. A count is something a supervisor selected or the platform recorded — exact. A reading is Flint reading the coaching conversations and what supervisors wrote, and judging what happened — an estimate, reviewed by a person before it reached this page. Survey ratings are counts, not readings.

The before-and-after surveys

Supervisors rated themselves on the same 0–10 questions at the start and the end of their cohort; managers rated each supervisor they oversee. A person counts only with a completed survey at both ends, compared question by question on the questions they answered both times; change is the percent change of the cohort average.

What this report does not claim

Nothing here is a claim about Terex’s own safety numbers. Incident rates, audit findings and near-miss reports are Terex’s, they move for many reasons, and connecting them to this work is Terex’s judgement. What this report covers is whether supervisors did the work, what the work produced in the moment, and what got in the way.
