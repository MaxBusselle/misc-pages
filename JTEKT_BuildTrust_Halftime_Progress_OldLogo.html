How the initiative works

Three skills, built one at a time.

Leaders work up through three moves: open with recognition, find the real cause of a Standard Work gap, then back it with data. Each one is practiced on a real moment on the floor.

Basic · Recognition
Lead with what's working
Name a specific thing an operator or Team Lead is doing right, before raising any gap.
Needed extra practice20%
Activities to move up2.5 avg

Most clear it cleanly; about one in five take a second pass.

Intermediate · Inquiry
Find the real cause
Keep asking past the first answer until the real barrier behind the gap surfaces, then get a commitment.
Needed extra practice14%
Activities to move up2.2 avg

Finding the cause comes fastest, with the fewest extra reps.

Advanced · Accountability
Back it with data
Use Bekido and cycle-time data to drive a clear fix, or an escalation to a specific person and date.
Needed extra practice36%
Still working it42 leaders

The hardest level: about a third take extra reps, as designed.

What "reached the highest level" meansThese leaders have completed the top-difficulty activities: backing a hard conversation with data and driving it to a fix or an escalation with a specific person and date. Captured & shared

The best practices leaders are teaching each other.

At the end of every activity, leaders write one tip for whoever does it next. 623 so far, about four per active leader: a coaching library JTEKT owns, in its own leaders' words. Ten of them, named plainly:

Recognize first
Don't jump straight into fixing problems. Point out something they're doing well first, then ask questions to understand what's really causing the issue before deciding on a fix.
Joshua Davis · Team Lead, Shipping
Let them talk
Ask open ended questions when dealing with frustrated people, because the more they talk the more you know and usually the better they feel because someone is listening.
Brandon Brooks · Team Lead
Trust the data
Dig into the data. Don't take people's word for it. That is not because they are dishonest. Sometimes reality isn't what you think it is until you look at the data.
Jeremy Crenshaw · Supervisor
Come in open
Go in open minded to talk with the second person and not having an opinion based off what the first person said with a fix already in place.
Jonathan Purvis · Supervisor, Production
Keep the line open
We as leaders should always try to have an open line of communication with the operators on the floor.
David Yount · Team Lead
Positive gets honesty
Pay attention to detail before approaching the problem and give them any positive feedback before getting to the root of the issue. You will get a more genuine response.
Daniel Bentley · Team Lead
Make praise specific
Just to make sure that the TL is actually letting the technician know what they are doing right. Not just a good job being done.
Amanda Martin · Supervisor, Production
Don't skip the wins
To always encourage positive feedback. Sometimes we all get so busy we tend to forget to stop and acknowledge when things are going well.
Sherry Weldon · Supervisor, Quality
Keep it casual
Don't make it into a task or job duty. Make it into a casual conversation to ease the tension or stress of doing something new.
Jeremy Rhoads · Supervisor, Die Maintenance
Start with questions
Ask the operators if they have any questions or concerns about anything at the start up meeting and not to just go over the not so good situations going on.
Gregory Derby · Team Lead
What we're learning

What your floor is telling us.

As leaders work through Standard Work gaps, the real causes are getting caught: a downed machine, a skipped step, a parts shortage. Across 710 sessions, 400 named a specific problem. Together they map what's actually breaking on the floor, built from the daily work itself, not a separate audit.

When a leader can't clear it alone · who it goes to

About 8% of conversations (60 of 710) surface a barrier bigger than the leader can fix. That's the range we'd expect: most gaps get resolved at the source, and only the ones beyond a leader's authority get handed off.

The pattern worth watchingEquipment faults and throughput are nearly half of what leaders surface: a live reliability signal from the floor. And 71 conversations were Standard Work simply not being followed: the exact gap this initiative was built to close, now visible and being worked.
