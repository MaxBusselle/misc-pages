Fresh Eyes Pilot: Results

Real Estate Operations August 2026 Flint Learning Solutions

Fresh Eyes ran with 43 Real Estate Operations colleagues from May 5 to July 17, 2026. Each week a participant took one short activity onto a branch visit already on their schedule, worked it through with an AI coach, and finished with a finding documented in FAMIS 360.

They completed 290 activities across 221 locations. Their managers rated them roughly two points higher after the pilot on documenting findings and surfacing issues that work orders and quality control inspections had not caught, on a 0 to 10 scale.

This document covers why Fresh Eyes was chosen as the first practice, what the pilot produced, the audiences proposed for the next wave, and the wider program map for REO.

01

Scope

Problem statement

Repeat blindness: a recurring issue stops registering because it has become part of the background. REO named this before the pilot began.

It is a habit problem rather than a knowledge problem. Facility managers know what a well-kept branch looks like, they have inspection checklists, and they have years in the role. What happens on a site visit is that attention goes to the reactive work order in hand, and the eye adjusts to whatever that location's baseline condition happens to be. The stained ceiling tile that has been there for two years reads as part of the building.

Inspection does not correct this on its own. An inspection measures the condition of a branch on the day someone looks. It does not change what the person who is there every month is capable of noticing on the days nobody is inspecting.

"They're all rushing to get their job done and overlooking things that they see every single day. It is a behavioral change and it has to start at the top."

Learning & Development Manager, Citizens REO
Source: REO pilot intake and discovery calls, March and April 2026.

Practice selection

Three practices were scoped for REO: observing conditions deliberately, coaching a team to the same standard, and holding vendors accountable. Fresh Eyes covers the first, and it was sequenced ahead of the other two for three reasons.

  • The other two practices depend on it. A manager cannot coach a team to a standard they are not applying themselves, and cannot hold a vendor to a standard they cannot describe. Observation is the base the other two stand on.
  • The hook already exists. Every facility manager has a branch visit coming this week and decides when it happens. Practice rides along with work that was going to occur anyway, so the pilot created no new meeting, no carved-out time, and no additional travel.
  • REO asked to start at the top. The pilot went to the managers who oversee maintenance and janitorial colleagues rather than to the frontline directly, on the reasoning that changing the eye of the person who sets the standard is what makes the standard travel.
  • It produces evidence for the next decision. Running the first practice with a defined group generates data on whether the mechanism works before anyone commits to the audiences behind it.
Source: Practice ranking, REO scoping documents, April 2026. Practice sequence confirmed with Citizens April 14, 2026.

Method

Flint builds a habit by having someone do the thing repeatedly, on real work, with attention on them while they do it. For Fresh Eyes that loop runs once a week and takes under ten minutes in the tool. The activity is the branch visit itself.

Delivery

One activity per week

Each activity attaches to a branch visit already on the participant's schedule. The participant opens it on the way in, works it on site, and closes it before leaving. Time in the tool is under ten minutes a week. The time on the branch is time they were spending anyway.

Coaching

AI coach during the activity

The coach runs the activity as a conversation while the participant is on site. It asks which location, then what was found, then where on the customer path it sits, then why it has persisted, then who owns the fix and by when. A general answer gets a follow-up question rather than an acknowledgement. The activity does not close until the finding is specific and documented.

Output

A record in FAMIS 360

The output is a real record against a real location, with a photo, an owner and a target date. No case studies and no simulations. The work the participant does inside the activity is work that would have needed doing regardless, which is why the pilot produced repairs as well as practice.

Measurement

Baseline and follow-up surveys

Participants rate themselves on five behaviors before the pilot and again at the end. Their managers separately rate each direct report on three. Both scales run 0 to 10 and are framed so that 5 means meeting expectations, not failing. Running both gives a self-report and an observed report on the same person.

Activity tiers

Activities are scaled from a ten-minute walkthrough to a full end-to-end audit, and participants move up at their own pace. The difficulty has to keep rising or the habit settles at noticing a scuff. Each tier adds one demand: the first asks the participant to see, the second to diagnose, the third to rank by customer impact and act.

Basic

Fresh Eyes Walkthrough

Ten to fifteen minutes on a scheduled branch visit. Parking lot, entrance, lobby, teller line, restrooms. Document at least one finding.

Result

42 completions, the highest-volume activity in the library. Every participant who completed anything started here. The tier proves the premise to the participant directly, which is more persuasive than being told it.

Intermediate

Catch the Repetitive Issue

Review 360 before the visit. Find a work order open past 60 days or an issue logged more than once. Categorize why it persisted.

Result

39 completions. This is where the record shifts from noticing to diagnosis. The activity will not close until the participant has assigned a category to why the issue kept coming back.

Advanced

End-to-End Quality Walk

Twenty-five to thirty-five minutes. Full customer path. Rank findings by what a first-time customer would notice. Act on the top item.

Result

23 completions, plus 25 of the Complete Customer-Eye Audit. Ranking by customer impact rather than by ease of fix is forced explicitly here, which is what separates this tier from the two below it.

Activities are scaled by difficulty from 1 to 10. One activity exists to test transfer: complete the work order you came for, then document at least one finding that has nothing to do with it. 43 of 48 attempts produced an unrelated documented finding, which is the cleanest measure in the pilot of the eye staying open after the task is done.

Twelve examples

Each of these comes from the coaching record for a single activity. They are selected for what happened after the finding rather than for the finding itself: an issue that had been stuck for months moving, a vendor being held to a date, a hazard removed, or a cause being named that changes how the next one is handled. Quotations are the participant's own words.

Root cause

Repairs applied to the wrong roof

The vestibule roof had been repaired more than once, and each time the leak returned and damaged the drywall ceiling below it. The participant brought his lead technician and checked the main roof set back behind the vestibule, which no previous work order had covered. The gap was there. The vestibule roof had never been the source, which is why every repair to it held for a while and then failed.

"There have been multiple work orders submitted to repair the small roof above the entry way but the water leak still happens... we found that the small roof was in good shape and not the cause."
WhitehouseFM Lead · Jun 3
Root cause

Water pressure open over a year

The pre-visit 360 review returned water pressure work orders recurring for more than twelve months, each one closed and each one reopened. The participant categorized it as a vendor gap: the fix was not holding because the cause had never been established. He redispatched the general contractor with a specific brief, to determine whether the restriction is internal or on the city side, and a target date of July 31 in place of another open-ended request.

"Issues has been happening for over a year. Redispatching my GC. With it being water pressure it could still be the City responsibility but they keep saying the pressure is fine on their end."
DearbornFacility Manager · Jun 4
Root cause

Tickets closed on one sensor reading

A temperature control issue had recurred since April. The participant's pre-visit hypothesis was the ordinary one, that nobody had pushed it. What he found on site was a closure rule: the control center was clearing these tickets on Building Monitoring System readings alone, which read normal on paper while the branch ran hot on the hottest days. The action step he logged names who has to change the closure standard, so the correction reaches past this branch.

"The control center often closes out the ticket using readings from the BMS system, which is only one metric. Not all avenues are explored or nuances investigated."
WoodmereSenior FM · Jun 12
Root cause

Drive-thru closed by recurring leaks

Ceiling leaks in heavy rain had closed the drive-thru. Several roofing tickets had already produced several repairs and the leaks continued, which the participant read as the roofers treating a symptom. Instead of opening another patch ticket he escalated the same day to the Senior Project Manager who runs roofing assessments, who engaged the vendor for a structural assessment. The documentation was consolidated under the existing loss event so the full history sits in one place.

"We have submitted multiple roofing tickets in which roofers have come out and make repairs but we still expereince leaks."
Airport PlazaFacility Manager · Jul 13
Vendor

Breaker open since May 1

The participant's schedule changed mid-activity, and he ran the pre-visit 360 sort on the replacement location anyway. It returned one item: an electrical circuit breaker replacement open since May 1. A call to the general contractor produced the reason for the delay, which was that the ticket had never surfaced in their queue. An electrician was scheduled for that week, and the photo, the owner and the target week went into the record.

"Open since 5/1/26. The GC had missed seeing this in their queue. TheY are scheduling electrician the week of 6/22."
HermitageFacility Manager · Jun 22
Vendor

Front doors open past 60 days

The front doors had been open past 60 days. The participant arrived with a hypothesis, that the delay was materials rather than vendor performance, and confirmed it on site. He did not leave it at that. His maintenance tech adjusted the doors so the entrance was safe in the interim, the contractor committed to an end-of-July delivery, and the branch, the regional manager and his own supervisor were all told where it stood.

"I had my maintenance tech adjust the doors so they work temporarily and are safe until the materials are delivered and the job can be completed. I called the contractor and the doors are coming in at the end of July."
BethelFM Lead · Jun 11
Vendor

Mulching deferred by the vendor

Annual mulching had not been performed, and the landscaping team's stated reason was that it was too early in the season. The participant went and looked at the beds. They needed it. Logging his own assessment with a photo, an action step and a target date turns a verbal explanation into a record, which is what the next conversation with that vendor rests on.

"The landscaping team says 'it is too early to mulch.' Mulch beds most definitely need freshening up."
BurgettstownFacility Manager · Jun 30
Vendor

ATM cleaning cadence changed

Two janitorial work orders in 90 days for the same thing, the face of the ATM. The participant categorized the cause as cleaning frequency against usage rather than vendor negligence: a 24-hour vestibule ATM in summer volume needs more than the standard rotation. He called his janitorial technician from site and secured an ongoing commitment to wipe the ATM face on every branch clean through the busy season. The outcome was a changed routine rather than a third ticket.

"I called my janitorial tech and asked him to wipe down the face of the ATM more often during the busy season... He agreed and will start each time he cleans the branch."
Peters TownshipFM Lead · Jun 11
Safety

Louver ranked above cosmetic finding

A recently renovated branch produced three findings: a missing lamp-post globe, a loose gable-vent louver, and a janitor sink left exposed in the men's room. Asked to rank them by customer impact, the participant set aside the one that looks worst for the one that could fall. He then escalated to the retail FM to press urgency, rather than leaving it to be picked up on the contractor's next batched visit.

"The globe is a bit ugly, but the louver could fall presenting a safety issue, so I will go with the louver is the top priority."
Washington St, Middletown CTFacility Manager · Jul 16
Safety

Cable across the teller line

The branch presented well and produced a single finding. The drive-thru card machine was being used at the teller line, with its cable stretched across the floor between the two positions. The participant removed the hazard during the visit by telling staff to walk the card over. He then handed the branch the permanent fix, an IT ticket to move the port to the teller line, because the workaround only holds while someone remembers it.

"I addressed it with the staff on the spot and told them even though it may be an inconvenience they will need to walk the card over for now. I suggested they put in a IT ticket to have the port moved to the teller line."
FlourtownFacility Manager · Jul 15
Outside scope

Abandoned dumpster explained a ticket

A Senior FM was on site with his team for a landscaping review, and had flagged an open trash accumulation work order in advance. Walking the property he found what was behind it: a dumpster left on the pad by the former hauler. Recording that cause against the recurring ticket changes it from a cleanup that repeats into a removal that ends it.

"We logged an old dumpster left onsite by the former trash hauler. This was also the reason for the trash accumulation around the dumpster reported in 360."
LimerickSenior FM · May 20
Outside scope

Shared-lot ownership determined

A parking lot repair had stalled. Nobody had refused it. The lot adjoins another business and no one had ever established who owned the affected area, so the work sat. The participant did that piece of work himself: he determined the area was the landlord's responsibility, contacted them directly, and got the repair scheduled for that week. The ownership finding and the timeline both went onto the work order.

"I have found out that the area within the parking lot that is need of repair is the Landlord's responsibility. I have contacted the Landlord and they are making the repair this week."
GlassboroFacility Manager · Jun 3

Best practice advice from participants

Every activity ends by asking the participant what they would tell another facility manager who was about to do the same thing. 263 of 290 activities produced an answer. Taken together they are a record of how this group works, written by the people doing it, and several of them describe methods that were not in the activity instructions. Nine examples.

"I would advise them to challenge themselves to look beyond the obvious, to ensure all aspects were explored and investigated. short sited fixes don't always lead to long-term solutions. 03

Next wave

Facility managers visit their locations. The four groups below are in the buildings far more often than that. Rover techs are on site at least twice a week, landscape leads move between sites and hubs, and the flooring crews work after hours when the branch is empty and there is more room to look. Extending Fresh Eyes to them puts the same observation habit in front of the colleagues with the most exposure to the estate.

All four groups work to a region rather than a fixed route, so activities can be aimed at a region that REO leadership wants improved. The quality control managers are the highest-leverage group of the four: at least one per region, and inspecting branches and moving work orders is already their job.

Facility managers stay in as evaluators rather than as new participants. They rate and validate their senior technicians and leads, which keeps the habit they built in the pilot in use and makes them the route by which the standard reaches their teams.

The activities are re-cut for these groups. Scope is established with the coach at the start of each conversation, so nobody is prompted on work outside their authority. Cadence flexes with workload and region rather than running to a fixed week, which matters for the flooring crews and the painters working nights. The full customer-path audit is reserved for the rover techs and the quality control managers.

"These are the people that are touching these buildings every day. They're in there at least once or twice a week... we need the engagement of the people who are there every day, because the facility managers, they're just not."

Learning & Development Manager, Citizens REO · July 15, 2026

Program map

Four business problems Flint and REO have discussed for Real Estate Operations. A program is a business problem that cuts across the organization and holds several initiatives over time. An initiative is one performance gap, run over roughly three months, with a defined audience and its own before-and-after measurement.

Fresh Eyes is one initiative inside Customer Focus. Everything else here is at idea stage, and the map is shared thinking rather than a committed plan. Its purpose is to show where the pilot sits and what the same mechanism could be pointed at once the audience and sequence are agreed.
