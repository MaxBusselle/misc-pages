Wentzville Pilot Update

September 11, 2026

Improve how team leaders perform the frontline role at Wentzville. Each participant gets short personalized activities in the flow of work, targeted at behaviors that significantly affect the business.

Four business challenges related to the team leader role at Wentzville
  1. Line stops that started as an earlier signal
  2. Defects created while an operator is behind or new
  3. Jobs run differently than the job instruction sheet
  4. Team leader effectiveness varying with the group leader
The pilot coaches four team leaders in Dave Elliott's area (Trim, IP/Glass) on behaviors that address them.
Overview 2 / 9
Business challenges and desired behaviors — 1 of 4

Line stops that started as an earlier signal

Team leader can be more proactive at early signs of problems.

Desired behavior
  • Work the line, not the desk.
  • Act on the andon tone and the blinking board.
Business challenges 3 / 9
Business challenges and desired behaviors — 2 of 4

Defects created while an operator is behind or new

An operator getting behind, or new to the station or build mix, increases the risk of jobs going down the line incomplete. What is not caught at the station becomes repair work later.

Desired behavior
  • Proactively work with the operator and get the job back on pace.
  • Stay with a new operator until the job holds.
Business challenges 4 / 9
Business challenges and desired behaviors — 3 of 4

Jobs run differently than the job instruction sheet

Desired behavior
  • Make sure operators run the job to the sheet.
Business challenges 5 / 9
Business challenges and desired behaviors — 4 of 4

Team leader effectiveness varies with the group leader

Different group leaders set different expectations. Team leaders change areas and are held to different standards.

Desired behavior
  • Run the team leader role to the highest priority written standards.
  • (example) Facilitate the start of shift and team meetings.
  • (example) Ready the team member's workstation and tools before production starts.
  • (example) Give team members quality feedback, and carry information across shifts.

Success criteria

From leadership perspective

  • Activities produce the intended outcome.
  • Participants execute the workplace practice.
  • Failed execution produces usable data.
  • Data surfaces new best practices and barriers.

From participant perspective

  • The right activities for the outcomes.
  • Workable in the flow of work.
  • A good use of their time.
