The program · What changed, and why

Rebuilt with the pilot's lessons.

The pilot was built for facility managers who own a branch. The rebuild changes real things so it fits people who work across the estate.

Across the board
Coaching, not audit
The tone asks what you noticed, not whether you found enough.
Safety in every activity
Walking surfaces, mats and trip hazards — named for every role, even with no work order.
Scope, not a clock
One zone or the full customer path — a published time never becomes a reason to skip a site.
A number or a photo
A work-order number where there is one; a photo where there isn't. The pilot couldn't verify photos alone.
Past your own trade
Log what a customer sees, not only your scope — where the pilot's cross-trade findings came from.
By role
Senior techs
Keys off the next dispatch; findings must be unrelated to the job; a P1 first, then a reset; an escalation path above their authority.
Landscape leads
The walk starts at the road; repeats counted in seasons, not days; findings land at the weekly huddle; still works in winter.
Flooring
The empty night building is the advantage; one zone, not the whole site; the point is what they see that isn't the floor.
Quality control
Re-walk a branch they've already scored; the customer path over the form; close the gap between a score and a work order.
The program · Facility managers

From doing the walks to coaching the team.

Facility managers ran the pilot. In the next wave they shift from doing the walks to validating and coaching.

1
They validate the frontline. When a tech reports a success, the manager gets a quick question to confirm it. A check, not a gate — it keeps managers close to the work and makes them the route the standard travels to the team.
2
They move to their own next initiative — Building Your Team's Quality Eye, coaching their team to the standard. Our recommendation for the second initiative, open to refinement with you.
3
New and existing managers go through the refreshed Fresh Eyes themselves. So they've done the frontline work before they coach it — and the three new managers get it as they join.
