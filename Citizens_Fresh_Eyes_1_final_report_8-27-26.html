The challenge

Familiarity leads to repeat blindness.

Covering 40–60 branches each, FMs stop noticing what a first-time customer sees. Quality issues go undocumented, recur, and get patched rather than fixed — while leadership sees only what QC and work orders surface.

Fresh Eyes gave FMs on-the-job activities to help them see every branch through a customer's eyes again. Three practices, each one a specific behavior an FM practices on a real visit:

Practice 1 · Basic
Walk with fresh eyes
Approach a branch like a first-time customer and document what you find in 360.

The practices in the field
One example of each practice, in the FM's own words.

Three practices, three FMs, three real visits — taken from the coaching conversations as they happened.

Practice 1 · Walk with fresh eyes
The activity: Focus on One ZonePick a single zone and walk it the way a first-time customer would.
I did not catch it on the way in, but on the way out I noticed the cover was off of the electric heater. I replaced the cover onto the heater and entered a work order to reflect the work done.
Newly refurbished branch · June 3
Alexander Maxwell · Facility Manager
The second look caught what the first walk missed — an exposed heater, fixed and logged before he left
Practice 2 · Catch the recurring issue
The activity: Catch the Repetitive IssueFind an issue that keeps coming back, name why it persists, and log an action step with an owner.
I realized the photocell has a metal piece that slid down and covered the eye. I was able to use a pole to push metal up and lights went off.
Exterior lights running in daylight · June 11
William Strobele · Facility Manager
Traced a repeat problem to its cause and cleared it on site — no vendor, no work order queue
Practice 3 · Audit from the customer's view
The activity: Complete Customer-Eye AuditWalk the full customer path, rank findings by customer impact, and act on the top one before leaving.
I opened a work order and contacted my janitorial tech, however he was on PTO. I then called his back up tech who was in the area and they are on they way now to do a deep cleaning.
Customer-visible finding · June 24
James Wright · Facilities Manager Lead
Did not log it and leave — got someone physically on the way before walking out
A standout story
A chronic problem, moving to solution.

Grove City had a roof leak over its ATM that had been coming back for years. Here is how that visit went, in the FM's own words.

Grove City, PA · June 18 · ATM canopy roof leak
The only thing that is reoccurring is a roof leak in the canopy above our ATM… It has reoccurred because the building is very old and nothing has been done with this roof [in] probably over 10 or 15 years.
Mark Marterella · Facility Manager · activity: Spot the Pattern Before You Walk In
What he did about it
I have already reached out to and issued a work order to our general contractor to have this roof evaluated and repaired to stop the roof leak.
The result: on site he confirmed the hypothesis — a drying, shrinking rubber membrane and compromised seams. The general contractor is now writing the evaluation and repair proposal. A leak above a customer-facing ATM, routed to its cause after 10 to 15 years of patching.
